THE PITCH — Tell it like a story

Opening (The Human Problem)

Imagine you're Ramesh. You're a fisherman in Veraval, Gujarat. You own a 10-metre motorised boat. You have a wife, two children, and a ₹15,000 monthly income that depends entirely on what you catch this week.

It's 4:30 AM. You need to decide — right now — whether to take your boat 20 kilometres into the Arabian Sea today.

Here's what you know: nothing. You can look at the sky. You can ask the guy at the next boat. That's it.

Here's what exists but you can't access: ISRO has a satellite watching the exact chlorophyll concentration at the spot you'd be fishing. IMD has a forecast model predicting 2.8-metre waves at that location at 11 AM. INCOIS has computed the potential fishing zone 18 km south-southwest of you. The Coast Guard knows there's a naval exercise zone 12 km east that you must avoid. All of this data exists. Right now. In a server somewhere.

But Ramesh can't read a chlorophyll map. He doesn't have a computer. He speaks Gujarati. His phone has intermittent 3G. And nobody has built the bridge between that satellite data and his decision at 4:30 AM.

535 fishermen were arrested last year for accidentally crossing an invisible line in the ocean — the maritime boundary — because no one told them the current was pushing them across it.

In Kerala alone, 469 fishermen have died at sea in 11 years. 54 in 2023. 49 in 2024. Karnataka: 57 in one year. Just two states. The national numbers are far higher but India doesn't even have a centralised way to count them.

That's the problem.

The data to save every single one of those lives already exists. It's just locked in formats that a fisherman on a boat can never access.

The Insight

We didn't need to build a new satellite. We didn't need new sensors. We didn't need new data.

We needed one system that takes all the fragmented pieces — the wave height from this model, the chlorophyll from that satellite, the warning from this bulletin, the boundary from that chart — and synthesises them into one answer, in one voice, in the fisherman's own language:

"Bhai, aaj mat jaao. 11 baje tak waves 3 metre hone wali hain. Kal subah 5 baje safe rahega — 18 km south-west mein mackerel milne ka chance 76% hai. Yeh raha route."

That's ORCA.

The Solution (One line)

ORCA is a multi-agent AI platform that ingests fragmented satellite and ocean data from 9 sources, runs it through 10 collaborative agents, resolves conflicts between sources using a strict authority hierarchy, and delivers one explainable, evidence-backed answer — spoken in the fisherman's own language.

One platform. Five stakeholders. Data that already exists. Lives that can be saved.

THE 12 INNOVATION USPs

These are the features that make ORCA genuinely different. Not incremental improvements — architectural innovations.

USP 1: Deterministic Safety Floors

No AI model, no LLM, no user input can ever lower a risk score past a safety floor. The system can only RAISE scores. When IMD issues a "do not venture" advisory, the floor locks at CAUTION minimum — regardless of what every other agent reports. This is not a feature. It's a safety guarantee.

USP 2: Hybrid Agent Architecture

2 LLM-powered agents handle language. 7 deterministic agents handle safety math. The LLM's job is to understand the question and explain the answer — it never touches the safety computation. Deliberate design, not a limitation.

USP 3: Evidence Ledger with Conflict Resolution

Every output carries its source, timestamp, confidence, and conflict history. When Open-Meteo says 2.1m waves and IMD says "high waves expected," the conflict resolver picks IMD (higher authority), preserves Open-Meteo's data as "overridden" with reason, and surfaces both to the user. Full audit trail.

USP 4: IMBL Current Guard

535 fishermen arrested in 2024 — not for deliberately crossing, but for being carried across by ocean currents. ORCA projects the vessel's position + current drift vector + time → predicts IMBL crossing 2 hours before it happens → alerts the fisherman to adjust course. A documented, quantified problem with a geometric solution.

USP 5: Drift Oracle (Pre-departure SAR Envelope)

Before the fisherman even leaves port, ORCA computes where his boat would drift if it lost power — using IMO standard leeway factors + live current + wind data. If the boat goes silent, the Coast Guard has an immediate search area. Currently, drift computation starts AFTER a vessel is reported missing (hours of delay). ORCA eliminates that delay.

USP 6: Peer-Reviewed Fishing Probability Model (HSI)

Not invented weights. Habitat Suitability Index — the standard methodology used by INCOIS, CMFRI, and published in peer-reviewed journals (Sarangi et al., 2024). Geometric mean of species-specific suitability curves for chlorophyll, SST, SST fronts, sea state, and crepuscular feeding patterns.

USP 7: Voice-First Multilingual Interface

Not voice as an afterthought — voice as the primary interface. Fisherman speaks in Hindi → Sarvam AI transcribes → agents process → answer is spoken back in Hindi. Designed for users who may not be able to read text on a small screen at sea.

USP 8: 10-Day Fishing Calendar

Same risk engine + fishing model, run on 10 days of forecast data. Output: "Best window this week: Thursday 5 AM – Friday 2 PM. Risk: 22/100. Fish probability: 78%." Lets fishermen plan their entire week — maximise catch, minimise risk, respect tidal windows.

USP 9: Vessels-at-Risk Counter for Disaster Management

When a cyclone is approaching: ORCA overlays the projected 48-hour impact cone with the active voyage registry → "17 vessels are currently inside the projected cyclone track." One number that could change how disaster response is deployed.

USP 10: Researcher Evidence API

Every query generates timestamped, geolocated evidence records. Researchers access them via REST API (GET /api/research/evidence), download as JSON/CSV/GeoJSON. Plus PFZ validation: submit catch reports → ORCA computes Skill Score and Hit Rate against its own predictions. ORCA improves from field data.

USP 11: 5nm Reserved Zone Geofencing

Mechanised trawlers routinely violate the 5-nautical-mile reserved zone for traditional fishers. ORCA logs violations with timestamp + vessel ID + location. First tool to give coastal authorities an enforceable, automated violation record.

USP 12: VCSS-Compatible Alert Format

ORCA generates structured alert payloads compatible with ISRO's Vessel Communication and Support System broadcast format. Post-selection, ORCA integrates directly with the 49,000+ transponders already deployed under PMMSY. The production path uses ISRO's own satellite infrastructure.

WHAT ORCA IS

ORCA is not just a weather app. It is not another SAMUDRA clone.

ORCA is an agentic data synthesis platform — it takes 9 fragmented data sources, runs them through a 10-module collaborative pipeline, resolves conflicts between sources using a strict authority hierarchy, and produces one explainable, evidence-backed answer, delivered in the user's language.

The key differentiator: ORCA can answer questions that no single source can answer alone: - "Should I go fishing at 6 AM tomorrow from Veraval and will I catch mackerel?" - "Will I accidentally cross the IMBL if I drift for 2 hours from my current position?" - "How many of my registered vessels are currently in the projected cyclone cone?"

These questions require simultaneously understanding weather, ocean physics, biology, geospatial boundaries, and natural language — that's the synthesis problem ORCA solves.

Cache Design

  • Key: (provider, round(lat, 2), round(lon, 2)) — ~1.1 km grid, reuses cache for nearby queries
  • TTL: 600s for success, 60s for failure (fast degradation to demo mode)
  • Thread-safe: threading.Lock + shared httpx.Client (pooled connections)

Evidence Normalisation

Every raw data point is normalised to a typed Evidence schema before entering the agent layer:

Evidence {
    metric, value, unit,
    source, source_type,         # "official_advisory" | "official_forecast" | "external_forecast" | "derived"
    observed_at, confidence,     # 0.0 – 1.0
    conflict_status,             # "selected" | "overridden"
    conflict_reason              # "" | "IMD advisory overrides Open-Meteo"
}

This is the atomic unit of data in ORCA. Nothing enters the agent layer without evidence wrapping.

LAYER 2 — THE 10-AGENT PIPELINE

Honest Framing: Hybrid Architecture

ORCA uses a hybrid agent architecture — deliberately: - 2 LLM-powered agents: Intent (NLU, language detection, query parsing) and Explanation (natural language generation, localised output) - 7 deterministic specialist agents: Weather, Ocean, PFZ, Cyclone, GIS, Risk, Route — typed functions computing safety-critical outputs - 1 orchestrator: Planner — manages the execution DAG, dispatches parallel calls

Why deterministic for safety agents? When lives are at stake, deterministic logic must own the safety decision. A language model's job is to understand the question and explain the answer — it must never touch the safety math.

LAYER 3 — APPLICATION SERVICES

Seven capabilities, all using data already flowing through Layers 0-2. No new data sources required.

1. Trip Decision Engine

"Should I go fishing at 6 AM from Veraval to catch mackerel?"

Combines: Risk score + Fishing probability + Route + Tidal state + Time of day → GO / CAUTION / NO-GO verdict with full explanation and evidence trail.

2. Fishing Probability Model (HSI-based)

Based on the peer-reviewed Habitat Suitability Index (HSI) methodology used in published fisheries literature (Sarangi et al., 2024, CMFRI; Zainuddin et al.):

HSI = (SI_chl^0.30 × SI_sst^0.25 × SI_front^0.20 × SI_sea_state^0.15 × SI_time^0.10)^(1/sum_weights)

Each SI (Suitability Index) is a 0–1 species-specific curve:

Factor Weight Scientific basis
Chlorophyll-a 0.30 Sarangi et al. (2024): strongest catch correlation — food proxy
SST band match 0.25 Thermal comfort — species-specific optimal range
SST front detection 0.20 Convergence zones — bait aggregation
Sea state 0.15 Operational constraint — rough water scatters shoals
Time of day 0.10 Crepuscular feeding peaks (5–7 AM, 5–7 PM) — documented in fisheries literature

Why HSI? It's the standard peer-reviewed methodology, used by INCOIS, CMFRI, and published in Environmental Monitoring and Assessment. The weights are defensible — they reflect the relative importance hierarchy established in published literature, not invented numbers.

3. Active Voyage Monitor

  1. Fisherman registers departure (POST /voyages) — stored in SQLite, survives server restarts
  2. GPS position updates every 5 min via navigator.geolocation.watchPosition() → queued in IndexedDB offline, synced on reconnect via Background Sync API
  3. Scheduler continuously checks conditions against all active voyages
  4. Detects deterioration: SAFE → CAUTION or CAUTION → UNSAFE
  5. Alerts only on worsening (no alert fatigue — stable conditions = silent)
  6. Triggers: Web Push notification + return route computation

4. Drift Oracle (Pre-departure SAR Envelope)

At voyage start, ORCA pre-computes where the vessel would drift if it loses power:

drift_position(t) = last_known_position
                  + ocean_current_vector × t          [from Open-Meteo Marine]
                  + 0.03 × wind_vector × t            [standard IMO leeway factor]
                  + stochastic_spread(σ increases with t)

Output: polygon on the map showing projected position at t+1h, t+2h, t+4h.

Why this matters: Currently Coast Guard starts computing drift AFTER a vessel goes silent (hours of delay). ORCA pre-computes this for every active trip. If a vessel stops responding, Coast Guard has an immediate search area.

5. IMBL Current Guard

535–560 Indian fishermen arrested in 2024 for inadvertently crossing the IMBL. The cause is ocean currents carrying them across — not deliberate intent.

ORCA solves this geometrically: 1. GIS agent provides distance to IMBL boundary polygon 2. Ocean agent provides current velocity + direction 3. Projection: if vessel continues at current speed + current drift, will it cross IMBL in 2 hours? 4. If yes → alert: "Ocean current is carrying you toward international waters. Adjust course 15° North."

This is a real documented problem with a purely geometric solution. No guesswork.

6. 10-Day Fishing Calendar

Runs the same risk engine + HSI fishing model on 10 days of Open-Meteo forecast data: - For each day × time slot (dawn, midday, dusk): computes risk score + fishing probability - Output: "Best window: Thursday 5 AM – Friday 2 PM (Safety: 22/100, Catch: 78%). Next best: Sunday morning." - Helps fishermen plan the entire week — maximize catch, minimize risk, respect tidal windows

7. Geofence Engine

Three types of geofences using the same ray-casting point-in-polygon math:

Zone Type Purpose

5 nautical mile reserved zones (state MFRA) Warn traditional fishers of boundary proximity; log mechanized vessel violations

  • This validation data feeds back into HSI weight calibration — ORCA improves from field data

3. Coastal Authority Interface (Governance Dashboard)

  • Vessel Registry: All registered vessels with live status (SAFE/CAUTION/UNSAFE) on map, filtered by district/zone
  • Geofence Violation Log: Timestamped record of mechanized vessels entering the 5nm reserved zone, exportable for enforcement
  • Monsoon Ban Compliance: During the 61-day ban — which vessels are active? Compliance % by district
  • Zone Risk Status: Aggregate risk level by coastal district, "15 vessels in HIGH RISK zone near Ratnagiri"

4. Disaster Management Interface (Situational Awareness)

  • Vessels-at-Risk Counter: When cyclone approaching — overlay projected impact cone with active voyage registry → "17 vessels currently inside the 48-hour cyclone projected track"
  • One-click Mass Alert: Push alert to all active vessels in a defined geographic zone — "Cyclone warning. Return to port immediately."
  • SAR Pre-Positioning Map: Drift envelopes for all active voyages overlaid on one map. If vessels go silent during a storm, Coast Guard sees exactly where to search first.
  • Coastal District Heatmap: Real-time risk heat map of entire coastline — where to pre-position rescue assets

5. Maritime Operator Interface (Fleet Operations)

  • Port Weather Windows: "Can we load containers at JNPT tomorrow at 6 AM?" — same risk engine, different vessel parameters (wave/wind thresholds differ for cargo operations)
  • Commercial Routing: A* route optimizer with commercial vessel parameters (20+ knots, deeper draft, different sea state tolerance)
  • Fleet Tracking: Multiple vessels tracked simultaneously with real-time condition overlay
  • ETA with Weather Delay: "Vessel Cochin → Chennai, ETA factoring current sea state" — route optimizer + wave-speed reduction model

OFFLINE ARCHITECTURE

A genuine architectural decision, not an afterthought.

3-Layer Offline Strategy

Layer A: Offline-First PWA (what the phone does with no signal) Before departure (at harbour with signal): - Service Worker (Workbox) caches full trip decision response, 24-hour forecast, map tiles for planned route area, port gazetteer, restricted zone polygons - Fisherman goes offline with complete trip intelligence cached - GPS continues working (satellite-based, no cellular needed) - App displays cached forecast with "Offline — cached from [timestamp]" banner

Layer B: Store-and-Forward GPS Queue - GPS positions logged to IndexedDB while offline - Background Sync API fires sync function whenever connectivity returns (even if app is closed) - Batch-uploads queued positions to PATCH /voyages/{id}/positions - Server reconstructs voyage track from batch upload

Layer C: Production Connectivity Path ISRO's VCSS (Vessel Communication and Support System) — an indigenous satellite transponder system — is already deployed on 49,000+ Indian fishing vessels (₹364 crore project, PMMSY). ORCA's alert engine generates structured alert payloads compatible with VCSS broadcast format. Post-selection, ORCA integrates directly with VCSS for two-way communication beyond cellular range — the same system that successfully reached vessels during Cyclone DANA (2024).

This is not hypothetical. VCSS exists, is operational, and ISRO built it. It's the natural production path for an ISRO problem statement.

ACCESS CONTROL & SECURITY

JWT-based role authentication: - POST /auth/login → returns JWT token with role field (fisher / researcher / authority / dm / operator) - FastAPI Depends() middleware validates token on protected routes - Authority dashboard requires role == "authority" in JWT — not a URL parameter - Passwords hashed with passlib (bcrypt). No external auth service needed.

STATE PERSISTENCE

Voyage state persists to SQLite — survives server restarts:

Data Storage Why
Active voyages (position, status, decision) SQLite voyages table Safety-critical — must survive restart
User sessions (last intent, language preference) SQLite sessions table UX continuity
Data cache (Open-Meteo, GDACS responses) In-memory only Ephemeral by nature, rebuilt from APIs on restart

Production upgrade path: Postgres + Redis — same SQL interface, no architecture change.

FUTURE ADDITIONS (What Comes After SIH Selection)

These are genuinely advanced capabilities, not incremental features. Each has a clear technical path and a real problem it solves.

1. NavIC/VCSS Deep Integration (ISRO-Specific)

India's NavIC satellite constellation provides more accurate positioning in Indian Ocean than GPS alone, and its L5-S dual band enables precise positioning even in adverse conditions. ISRO's VCSS transponders — already on 49,000 vessels — use NavIC for positioning and satellite messaging.

Post-selection ORCA: Registers as a VCSS application provider, receiving position beacons directly from transponders (bypassing cellular entirely) and pushing ORCA-computed alerts through the VCSS broadcast channel. This solves the connectivity problem permanently for the 49,000 vessels already equipped.

Why this matters for the pitch: the problem setter (ISRO) built this system. Saying "our production deployment integrates with your VCSS infrastructure" is the right answer.

2. Species-Specific Adaptive Fishing Zones

Current ORCA computes one fishing probability score. Advanced ORCA: species-specific HSI models.

Different target species have different optimal conditions: - Pomfret: 26–28°C, mid-water, monsoon-adjacent - Mackerel: 22–27°C, surface, highly chlorophyll-dependent - Tuna: 28–30°C, deep water, offshore, SSH anomaly-driven - Hilsa: Brackish water interface, estuarine, salinity-dependent

Fisher selects "I'm targeting pomfret" → ORCA runs the pomfret-specific HSI curve set → optimal zone is biologically calibrated for that species. Validated against OBIS species occurrence data per region.

3. LoRa Mesh Network (Vessel-to-Vessel Communication)

Small LoRa radio modules (₹500–₹1500, ESP32 + SX1278) attached to the boat: - Boats within 10–15 km of each other form an ad-hoc mesh - Position updates and condition reports relay through the mesh toward shore (multi-hop) - If vessel A is within cellular range, it relays vessel B's position to shore - Emergency SOS relay: nearest vessel receives alert and relays to shore - Works completely offline — no infrastructure required - Meshtastic protocol provides proven implementation

This permanently solves the connectivity gap for fishing fleets that operate in groups (common in Indian small-scale fishing). Cost per vessel: ~₹800.

4. Marine Pollution Sentinel

The same satellite data (SST, chlorophyll) that powers fishing predictions can detect environmental anomalies:

  • Oil spills: Anomalous chlorophyll depression in a pattern inconsistent with weather or tides
  • Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs): Chlorophyll spike with unusual spectral signature
  • Industrial thermal discharge: SST anomaly near port areas with no meteorological explanation
  • Dead zones: Correlated oxygen-depletion signatures (SST, chlorophyll correlation break)

ORCA runs continuous anomaly detection and alerts researchers and coastal authorities automatically. Extends ORCA from a fishing advisory platform to a marine ecosystem health monitoring system — directly addressing the "Marine Ecosystem Reasoning" in the PS title.

5. Predictive SAR (Search and Rescue Pre-Positioning)

Beyond reactive SAR (respond after a vessel goes silent), ORCA generates proactive SAR readiness intelligence:

  • Analyses historical distress call patterns (location, time, conditions, vessel type)
  • Identifies statistically high-risk corridors for each season, weather pattern, moon phase
  • Generates a daily "SAR readiness heatmap" for Coast Guard: "These zones are high-risk today — pre-position assets here before incidents occur"
  • Fleet-level analysis: if 50 vessels are at sea during a developing storm, computes the most statistically likely incident locations for pre-positioning

Transforms Coast Guard from reactive to predictive.

6. Marine Ecological Health Index

Monthly ecological assessment of key fishing grounds: - Is this fishing ground being overfished? (catch reports vs biomass estimate from chlorophyll trends) - Chlorophyll productivity trend over years — is this ground recovering or declining? - Coral reef health indicators from SST anomaly duration (bleaching risk) - Mangrove cover change (affects coastal nurseries for juvenile fish) - Output: Fishing Ground Health Score per region — supports sustainable fishing regulation

7. Fish Market Intelligence + Optimal Landing Port

Real-time fish landing prices from FISHCOPFED and NaFDB (publicly available): - "Sardine price at Sassoon Docks today: ₹85/kg, Versova: ₹70/kg" - Route optimizer considers both safety AND market destination - Price trend prediction based on incoming catch volumes from voyage registry - Connects the fisherman's economic decision to the safety decision — unified recommendation
